### Understanding Your Rights Before You Start

Before you dive into the legal filing guide, it's good to know a few basic legal ideas. This helps you understand what lawyers are talking about and what to expect. Knowing your rights empowers you during this process.

#### Statute of Limitations
The "statute of limitations" is a legal time limit. It means you only have a certain amount of time from when your injury happened to file a lawsuit. If you wait too long, you might lose your chance to get compensation. This time limit can be different depending on where you live and the type of claim you have.

For example, in many states, you might have two or three years for a personal injury claim, but medical malpractice claims might have different rules. It's really important to find out what your specific time limit is. Morgan & Morgan lawyers can tell you exactly what the statute of limitations is for your particular situation.

#### Types of Damages
When you file a claim, you are asking for "damages." Damages are the money paid to you to make up for your losses. There are different kinds of damages that a claim can cover.

Common types include economic damages, like money for your medical bills and lost wages. Non-economic damages cover things like pain and suffering or emotional distress. In some rare cases, punitive damages might be awarded to punish the person who caused the harm.

#### Step 5: Discovery Phase

After the lawsuit is filed, both sides enter the "discovery" phase. This is a period where both parties exchange information and evidence. It’s like both sides laying out their cards on the table. This helps prevent surprises at trial and encourages fair settlements.

##### What Is Discovery?
Discovery involves formal ways to get information from the other side. This can include written questions, requests for documents, and taking sworn statements from witnesses. It's designed to gather all relevant facts.

The goal is to understand the strengths and weaknesses of both sides of the case. This transparency helps everyone prepare for what might happen next. It helps in assessing the value of your injury claim steps.

##### Interrogatories and Depositions
Two common parts of discovery are "interrogatories" and "depositions." Interrogatories are written questions that one party sends to the other, who must answer them in writing under oath. Depositions are live, in-person question-and-answer sessions where a witness gives sworn testimony outside of court, usually with a court reporter present.

You might be asked to give a deposition, where the other side's lawyer will ask you questions about your injury and how it has affected your life. Your morgan and morgan lawyers will prepare you thoroughly for this. They will make sure you understand what to expect and how to answer questions truthfully and effectively.
